开场：正在变化的世界秩序Opening: a changing world order

主持人（Tano Santos）：过去30年世界变了。我们年轻时，世界是开放的、跨国界的，有一种如今似乎缺失的"精神上的慷慨"。你如何看待未来的走向？这会进入你对投资的思考吗？

Tano Santos: The world has changed over the last 30 years. When we were younger it was open, transnational, with a certain generosity of spirit that seems lacking today. How do you see it going forward, and does it enter into how you think about investing?

李录：这是个好问题，Tano。过去30年世界确实变了，这毫无疑问。但"世界如何自我治理"这类大问题的变化是缓慢的——一种秩序的形成与转变，可能需要十年，有时上百年。当前的秩序是经由两次世界大战锻造出来的：那两场战争把世界推到崩溃边缘，之后形成了美国主导的秩序，再经过冷战及其终结，变成一个统一的秩序——而现在，它又在转变为某种尚未可知的形态。

Li Lu: It's a great question, Tano. The world really has changed over the last 30 years — no question. But the big questions of how the world governs itself change on a slow scale; it can take a decade, sometimes a hundred years, for an order to form and transform. The current order was forged through two world wars that brought the world to the brink of collapse, emerged as the American-led order, then through the Cold War and its end became one order — and now it's transforming once again into something yet to be seen.

政治之下：经济秩序Beneath politics: the economic order

在政治之下，还有另一种秩序，过去几百年里一直在稳步形成——经济秩序。当越来越多的国家和人口加入一个共同市场、彼此交易时，它就形成了。这个共同市场大约在30年前最终形成：随着苏联解体、冷战结束，几乎所有国家都加入了同一个市场经济。

Underneath, another order has been steadily forming for the last few hundred years — the economic order. It forms when more and more countries and populations join a common market and transact with each other. That common market was finally formed about 30 years ago, with the collapse of the Soviet Union and the end of the Cold War, when virtually all countries joined one market economy.

一旦共同市场形成，任何单一国家都很难主动退出，因为市场带来效率，带来持续改进的飞轮——技术、分工、资源的交易。长期退出代价极大。看看过去100年的贸易摩擦：无论你设立什么壁垒，人们都会通过第三国、按同样的比较优势原则找到交易的办法。以中美贸易为例：它确实下降了，但看美国与墨西哥等国的整体贸易，会出现一个几乎等量的对冲——贸易基本上通过第三方延续了下来。

Once a common market is formed, it's very difficult for any single country to withdraw voluntarily, because out of the market comes efficiency and a flywheel of continuous improvement — technology, division of labor, trade in resources. Withdrawing for any extended period comes at great cost. Look at trade frictions over the past 100 years: no matter what barriers you erect, people find a way to trade through a third country along the same principles of comparative advantage. Take US–China trade: it has declined, but if you look at overall US trade with, say, Mexico, there's an exact offset — the trade essentially continues through a third party.

政治秩序方面：美国领导全球80年，承担了维持共同市场运转的大部分成本，如今开始觉得成本与收益不成比例，于是开始主动退出一部分责任——目前并不很成功——留下了真空。而最大的变化，是中国在过去30到40年间崛起为一个庞大的经济体，拥有完整的制造与技术自给能力，以及一个巨大、开放、监管统一的单一市场。

The political order is that the US, having led the globe for 80 years and shouldered most of the cost of keeping the common market functioning, began to feel the cost and benefit were out of proportion, and started to voluntarily withdraw some of those responsibilities — not very successfully so far — leaving a vacuum. The single biggest change is the rise of China over 30–40 years into an enormous economic entity, with a complete set of manufacturing and technology self-sufficiency and a single, large, open, unified-regulation market.

05:29

相互依赖——"筷子"段子Mutual dependence — the "chopsticks" point

我们在同一个市场内的相互依赖只增未减。举个例子：在生成式AI里，大家都离不开的东西是半导体芯片。想想所有能生产芯片的地方——它们有一个共同点。所有生产这波技术浪潮所必需芯片的国家和人……都用筷子。

Our mutual dependency within one single market has only increased. Example: in generative AI, the one thing everybody needs is semiconductor chips. Think about all the places that produce them — they have one thing in common. Every country and every person who produces the semiconductor chips essential to this technological wave… uses chopsticks.

当然，领导这里半导体公司的人——从黄仁勋到苏姿丰，不胜枚举。所以存在巨大的相互依赖：如果你像现在这样重注押在这项新技术上，无论它有什么希望与风险，你都得对用筷子的人友好一点。这不是偶然发生的。

And of course the people leading the semiconductor companies here — from Jensen Huang to Lisa Su, you name it. So there's a great deal of mutual dependence: if you're betting as heavily as we are on this new technology, whatever its promise and perils, you have to be nice to people who use chopsticks. This didn't happen by accident.

美国总统和中国领导人之所以会在8个月里见四次面，是有原因的——而每次会面最重要的成果，就是约定下一次会面。这就是"战略稳定"：所有人都感受到这个共同市场的分量和退出的代价，于是在激烈竞争的同时维持和平，各自都相信时间站在自己这边——中国要完成半导体自给，美国则寄望于AI突破。二战后的秩序正在改变，但更深层的——全球单一共同市场的形成——会长久存在。

There's a reason the President of the United States and the President of China found the urgency to meet four times in eight months — and the most important accomplishment of each meeting is to schedule the next one. This is strategic stability: everyone feels the weight of the one common market and the cost of withdrawing, so they keep it peaceful while competing fearlessly, each believing time is on their side — China to complete semiconductor self-sufficiency, the US hoping for an AI breakthrough. The post-WWII order is changing, but the deeper formation of one single global common market is here to stay.

对投资者的含义：六条原则Implication for investors: six principles

对投资者意味着什么？回到价值投资的六条原则。我们新加的最后一条：作为全球投资者，你的财富，表现为你对全球购买力的占比。实现它的一种方式——也是我们一直实践的方式——是在有活力的国家和市场里，以有利的价格买入伟大的企业并长期持有，从而创造价值，保持并提升我们的占比。

What's the implication for an investor? Back to the six principles of value investing. The last one we added: as a global investor, your wealth is expressed as your percentage ownership of global purchasing power. One way to accomplish that — the way we've practiced — is to buy great businesses in dynamic countries and markets at advantageous prices and hold them long term, to create value and preserve and grow our percentage over time.

11:25

"教堂旁边的赌场""A church with a casino attached"

回到巴菲特的那个比喻：股市是一座教堂，旁边附带一个赌场。伯克希尔60年来所做的，是把那座教堂建成了一座宏伟的大教堂——它既发挥功能，也让人们获得精神慰藉，因为它提供了市场之所以存在的那个正当功能。而我们30年来想证明的，是这套"福音"可以传播——我们能在别的国家建起教堂。我们选择在亚洲和其他地区去建；随着时间推移，其中一些教堂也可能长成大教堂。

Back to Warren's analogy describing the stock market as a church with a casino attached to it. What Berkshire has accomplished over 60 years is to build that church into a gigantic cathedral — one that functions, and where people can find spiritual solace, because it provides the right function the market exists for. What we've tried to practice over 30 years is to prove the gospel can be spread — that we can build churches in other countries. We chose to build in Asia and other regions; over time, some of those churches may grow into cathedrals too.

13:14

抉择：创造价值 vs 零和The choice: value creation vs. zero-sum

"教堂旁的赌场"是基本格局，这源于人性——但两边对社会的意义截然不同。一边是正和的方式，对社会是净收益；另一边是零和游戏，是净成本、净负面影响。选择权在你。而技术，包括AI，应当是工具，而非替代品——如果它倾向于替代人类，我们都会联合起来反对去创造一个"人造的神"。所以你完全不必参与那种可以称之为"半神IPO"的东西。

The church-with-a-casino is the basic setup because of the human condition — but the two sides have very different implications for society. On one side, a win-win approach and net benefit to society. On the other, a zero-sum game, a net cost, a net negative. The choice is yours. And technology, including AI, should remain a tool, not a replacement — if it tends toward replacing humans, we'll all unite to oppose creating a man-made god. So you don't have to participate in what amounts to the "IPO of a demigod."

市场上的四类参与者Four kinds of market participants

我把这个问题分成两部分——先讲投资者在做什么，再讲AI。市场上其实只有四类人：

Let me split this into two parts — first what investors do, then AI. In the market there are really only four kinds of people:

  • 价值投资者——长期看重企业本身，为的是价值创造。
  • 套利者——对市场定价的对错进行套利；用本·格雷厄姆的比喻，像房地产一样，从悲观者手里买入，卖给乐观者。
  • 投机者——主要为投机而来，猜别人会买或卖什么，既不关心价值、也不关心定价或价值创造。
  • 经纪商与做市商——为上述所有人提供便利。
  • Value investors — taken with the company over time, for value creation.
  • Arbitrageurs — arbitraging the rights and wrongs of market pricing; in Ben Graham's analogy, like real estate, buying from the pessimists and selling to the optimists.
  • Speculators — in it mostly for speculation, guessing what others will buy or sell, caring about neither the value, the pricing, nor the value creation.
  • Brokers and market makers — who facilitate all of the above.

大多数人并非纯粹的某一类——他们或多或少兼具这四种；指数化恰恰就是这个平均。但价值投资者真正在意的，是获得正确的价格发现，并等待价值随时间被创造出来。

Most people aren't purists — they encompass somewhat all four; indexing is exactly that average. But value investors are into getting the right price discovery and waiting for value creation over time.

从社会角度看：投机，按定义就不会停留足够久以参与任何价值的变化或创造，所以它大致净为零——而且是对市场运转的一种成本。市场之所以存在，主要是为了把过剩的储蓄导向有价值企业的生产性用途，创造一个具有复利增长的现代经济。那为什么还会有投机这一面？这是人性的一部分；赌场几乎是"建筑许可"的一部分。400年前人们靠它被"动物精神"点燃，400年后依然如此。所以，作为学生，你的选择是：零和游戏，还是价值创造游戏？这是个人选择——社会也有选择：不要让赌场那一面大到压过市场存在的本来目的。

Socially: speculation, by definition, doesn't stay long enough for any value change or creation, so it nets to roughly zero — and is a cost to the functioning of the market. The market exists primarily to channel excess savings to productive use in deserving companies, creating a modern economy with compound growth. So the question is why you even have the speculative side — it's part of the human condition; the casino is almost part of the "building permit." 400 years ago that's how you got inspired by animal spirits, and 400 years later it still does. So the choice, as a student, is: zero-sum game, or value-creation game? That's personal — and society also has a choice, not to let the casino side grow so out of proportion that it overwhelms why the market exists.

什么是AI——工具还是造物What is AI — tool vs. creature

接下来谈AI——一个更复杂的问题。这波生成式AI显然是一次巨大的技术浪潮。自工业革命以来我们经历过一波又一波，懂得"创造性破坏"。但这一波让人感觉格外不同。表面上：变化的速度、涉及的规模，以及高度集中于顶端的资金冲击，都是前所未有的。

On to AI — a more complex question. This generative-AI wave is obviously a huge technological wave. We've dealt with waves since the Industrial Revolution, understanding creative destruction. But this one feels unusually different. On the surface: the pace, the scale, and the concentrated financial impact at the top are unprecedented.

为什么？在此之前，我们一直把技术当作提升人类生产力的根本工具。而这项技术引入了一个不同的元素：现在我们处在"工具 vs 造物"的光谱上，并且已经开始沿着它移动。大约4年前它首次出现时，是一个"图灵测试"时刻——仅凭语言，你已无法分辨那是机器还是人。此后出现了推理；从推理到编程；有了编程能力，它成为能像人一样自主执行的智能体；然后是众多智能体在复杂、长期任务上的协作（即"长推理"），用它们自己的沟通工具自我组织。

Why? Until now we dealt with technology as a fundamental tool for human productivity. This technology introduces a different element: now we're on a spectrum of tool versus creature, and we've begun to move along it. The moment it was introduced ~4 years ago was a Turing-test moment — from language you could no longer tell machine from human. Then came reasoning; from reasoning, coding; with coding, autonomously executing agents functioning much as a human does; then collaboration of agents on complex, long-lasting tasks ("long reasoning"), self-organizing with their own communication tools.

硅谷的叙事是 RSI → AGI → ASI——智能水平超越所有人类个体乃至整体——于是你是在"创造一个半神"，一个人造之物，强大到其经济影响必然巨大，从而为所有投入提供了正当理由。于是就有了那个邀请所有人参与的万亿美元IPO——"神的IPO"。

The Silicon Valley narrative runs RSI → AGI → ASI — intelligence exceeding all humans individually and together — so you're "creating a demigod," a man-made creation so powerful its economic impact must be huge, justifying all the investment. Hence the trillion-dollar IPO you're all invited to — "the IPO of a god."

但退一步想：如果真是如此，我们将生活在一个被人造神统治的社会里，这个神只有极少数人能接触、无人能理解、无人能控制。没有人愿意生活在那样的社会。所以只有两种结局：（A）这项技术根本没有朝那个方向发展；或者（B）如果真朝那走，我们都会在某个时点联合起来阻止它。无论哪种情况，都不会出现一个凌驾于人类社会之上的人造神。

But step back: if that were truly the case, we'd be living in a society lorded over by a man-made god, accessed by very few, understood by none, controlled by nobody. Nobody wants to live in that society. So there are only two outcomes: (A) the technology isn't actually progressing in that direction, or (B) if it is, we'll all unite at some point to stop it. In neither case do we end up with a man-made god ruling over human society.

25:34

给AI一个临时定义A provisional definition of AI

它是有用的技术吗？基本上，是的。我给一个临时定义：它是一台机器，通过施加庞大算力和聪明的算法，用全部人类生成的数据，在近乎无限的变量之间建立系统性的、情境化的关联——是人类生成数据的"一阶导数"——并在此过程中获得了包括大量人类推理与智能在内的能力。目前我们知道它如何运作、如何改进，却并不完全明白它"为什么"有效——就像我们也并不完全明白人体这台有机机器如何运作。

Is it a useful technology? Reasonably, yes. My provisional definition: it's a machine that, by applying brute compute and clever algorithms over all human-generated data, establishes a systematic, contextual correlation among near-infinite variables of that data — a first derivative of human-generated data — and in doing so acquires capabilities including much of human reasoning and intelligence. We know how it works and how to improve it, but not fully why it works — just as we don't fully understand how the human organic machine works.

它把经济的某一层"拉平"。设想经济的结构：底层是能源与交通；之上是制造业；再往上是通信与数字媒体；最顶层，是把人类积累的全部知识加以综合、服务于各层经济的庞大的知识型、技术型服务。今天的生成式AI，是综合并拉平最顶层知识与专长的绝佳工具——但它改变不了能源与交通，也改变不了制造业（本质是分子和原子的组合），而通信与数字化本身已是最强的综合者。所以它尚未超越人类；我们仍牢牢处在人类生成数据的范围内。

It equalizes one layer of the economy. Picture the economy: at the bottom, energy and transportation; above that, manufacturing; above that, communication and digital media; and on top, the enormous knowledge- and technology-based services that synthesize accumulated knowledge to serve every layer. Generative AI today is a great tool to synthesize and equalize knowledge and expertise in that top layer — but it can't change energy and transportation, or manufacturing (combining molecules and atoms), and communication/digitization are already the greatest synthesizers. So it hasn't gone beyond humans; we're still firmly within human-generated data.

29:38

那条红线The red line

总结一下：这项技术极其有用、极具变革性，同时也极其危险。作为社会、作为人类整体，我们必须坚定地划出一条红线——它的任何自我学习或使用，都不能脱离"人在环中"；要把它牢牢守在工具一侧，而非造物一侧。没有人在环中的RSI（递归式自我改进）会非常危险，因为它会生成自己的目标和子目标，逐渐偏离人类的控制。它同样极具颠覆性——从就业，到情感依赖，再到网络、生物、核等各类风险。社会应追求一种平衡的发展节奏，在收益与风险之间取得平衡。

In summary: this technology is enormously helpful and transformative, and extraordinarily dangerous. As a society and as human beings we must firmly establish a red line — that its learning and use cannot be without a human in the loop; keep it firmly on the tool side, not the creature side. RSI (recursive self-improvement) without a human in the loop would be very dangerous, because it would develop its own goals and sub-goals and drift beyond human control. It's also enormously disruptive — from employment to emotional dependency to cyber, bio and nuclear risk. Society should aim for a balanced pace of development that balances benefit and risk.

另外，和互联网及许多一次性投入的技术不同，这项技术始终带有增量成本——所以收入、利润、资本需求与财务纪律将始终起作用，并受社会容忍度的节制。人类社会的适应，从来都是通过"规划"来完成的——我们会经历巨大的繁荣与崩溃周期，也许不止一轮。

Also, unlike the internet and many prior technologies with a one-time investment, this technology always carries incremental cost — so revenue, profit, capital needs and financial discipline will always be in operation, paced by society's tolerance. How society adapts is always by planning — and we'll go through big boom-and-bust cycles, perhaps several.

33:46

能力圈；到有鱼的地方钓鱼Circle of competence; fish where the fish are

投资是对一个本质上不可预测的未来进行预测，所以你需要安全边际，既保护下行，也为上行留出空间。沃伦又加了一条纪律：能力圈——而其中最重要的，是认清它的"边界"，因为大多数人都以为自己比实际更有能力一点。查理的说法是：到有鱼的地方去钓鱼。有些市场、行业或企业吸引了大量关注和竞争，竞争吃掉了你的回报；另一些则关注不足，反而给你多得多的机会。道理很简单，实践中却异常有效。

Investing is predicting an inherently unpredictable future, so you require a margin of safety that protects the downside and leaves room for upside. Warren added the discipline of the circle of competence — and the most important part is knowing its edge, because most of us think we're a little more capable than we are. Charlie's idea: fish where the fish are. Some markets, industries or businesses draw huge attention and competition that eats your returns; others don't attract enough attention and offer much more. A simple idea, amazingly effective in practice.

54:20

市场会做怪事；长期回报Markets do strange things; long-run returns

主持人（Tano Santos）：AI资本开支占GDP的比重，历史上是2%–4%，如今因为英伟达到了约15%。你如何看待一个过度依赖少数高beta公司的市场？

Tano Santos: AI capex normalized by GDP, historically 2–4%, is now ~15% with Nvidia. How do you think about a market overly dependent on a few high-beta companies?

李录：市场总会做出奇怪的事——你不必被它牵着走。这是本·格雷厄姆最重要的贡献之一。设想一家公司，自称是研究实验室，烧掉数百亿、还承诺继续烧掉数千亿，承诺将变革技术——但按它自己的说法，又有10%的概率毁灭所有人——而在同一份招股说明书里，它邀请所有人以2万亿估值至少投入1000亿美元。这是市场有多非理性的一个极端例证。

Li Lu: The market can always do peculiar things — you don't have to be dictated to by it. That's one of Ben Graham's most important contributions. Consider a company that calls itself a research lab, burns tens of billions and promises to burn hundreds of billions, promises to transform technology — but also carries, by its own account, a 10% chance of killing everyone — and in that same prospectus invites everyone to put in at least $100 billion at a $2 trillion valuation. That's an extreme illustration of how irrational the market gets.

市场在很长的时段里会做奇怪的事。标普自2010年起16年年均约14%–15%；而2000到2010那十年年均几乎为零，实际略为负。把过去26年平均一下，得到的是一个"高个位数"回报——这大致就是长期的正常水平（约2%的GDP增长 + 2%–3%的通胀 ≈ 4%–5%的名义增长，而500家最优秀公司略好于此）。这应当成为你的根基性支柱：长期看，你终将得到你应得的；几乎没有免费的午餐。这种两极分化，反而给理性的头脑提供了比平常更好的机会集——前提是你能保持理性，而这非常困难，尤其当一波大的技术浪潮把想象力拉得很长、投机的一面变得活跃而狂野时。

Markets do strange things over long stretches. The S&P from 2010 averaged ~14–15% over 16 years; from 2000–2010 it averaged essentially zero, slightly negative. Average the last 26 years and you get a high-single-digit return — which is about right long term (≈2% GDP growth + 2–3% inflation ≈ 4–5% nominal, with the 500 best companies doing slightly better). That should be your grounding pillar: over the long run you get what you deserve; there's not much of a free lunch. This bifurcation offers rational minds a better opportunity set than normal times — if you can stay rational, which is very difficult, especially when a big technology wave stretches imaginations and the speculative side runs wild.

1:01:50

中国：是"目标"还是"代价"China: objective vs. cost

中国股市停滞了大约10年，但底下发生的事很耐人寻味。十年前，中国面对"中等收入陷阱"的问题——数十个国家到了这个水平后被困40到50年。于是中国有意把经济从中低端制造迁移到中高端制造——一个十年计划，"中国制造2025"。随后第一任特朗普政府的贸易战、以及拜登政府加码的技术限制，反而强化了这一推进；到2021年，中国加倍下注，并在瓶颈领域（半导体、大飞机发动机、操作系统软件等）加上了技术自给的目标。

The Chinese market was stagnant for about 10 years, but what happened underneath is fascinating. A decade ago China faced the middle-income-trap question — dozens of countries reach that level and stay stuck for 40–50 years. So they deliberately migrated the economy from low/mid-end to mid/high-end manufacturing — a 10-year plan, "Made in China 2025." Then the first Trump administration's trade war, and the Biden administration's added technology restrictions, reinforced the push; by 2021 China doubled down and added technology self-sufficiency in bottleneck areas (semiconductors, aircraft engines, operating software, etc.).

实现这些目标付出了沉重代价——主要落在中产家庭的资产负债表上。2021年房地产见顶；一个典型的中产家庭约70%的净资产系于住房，而这套房又背着约50%的按揭杠杆。此后五年，全国房价平均下跌约40%，于是家庭财富平均缩水约60%–70%。人们的应对是三件事：少消费、多储蓄、尽量还债——于是内需增长放缓到接近零，储蓄率不断上升（存款约165万亿元人民币）。这是经济的一层。

Achieving those goals carried a heavy cost — chiefly the middle-class family balance sheet. By 2021 real estate peaked; a typical middle-class family had ~70% of net worth in a home carrying ~50% mortgage leverage. Over the next five years home prices fell ~40% nationwide, so family fortunes dropped ~60–70%. People responded by consuming less, saving more, and paying down debt — so domestic demand slowed toward roughly zero and the savings rate climbed (deposits ~165 trillion RMB). That's one tier of the economy.

另一层：制造业已稳固地突破到中高端，"中国制造2025"的每一项目标都提前完成，瓶颈也在缓解。事实证明，美国主导的制裁对中国半导体产业是"天赐之物"——它带来了市场经济中最珍贵的东西：需求。订单→收入→利润→研发→改进→更多收入。在成熟制程上，设备端和制造端已接近完全自给；在最尖端制程上也日益接近，只差几种关键要素。生成式AI的代码生成能力，帮助替换了缺失的软件层。与此同时的代价是：新房年销售额下降约70%，而几乎同等规模的约每年10万亿元人民币，被投入到制造业升级和技术上——这一分化恰好出现在2021年前后。

The other tier: manufacturing broke firmly into mid/high-end, every "Made in China 2025" objective is ahead of schedule, and bottlenecks are easing. US-led sanctions turned out to be a godsend for China's semiconductor industry — they created demand, the most precious thing: order book → revenue → profit → R&D → improvement → more revenue. On mature nodes, equipment and fabrication are near self-sufficient; on cutting-edge nodes they're getting close, with a few missing ingredients. AI's code-generation helps replace missing software layers. Meanwhile the cost: new real-estate sales down ~70%, and roughly the same ~10 trillion RMB/year shifted into manufacturing upgrading and technology — the divergence appears right around 2021.

这就是发展模式：大约每10年设定一组新目标，并伴随沉重代价；而这一阶段的"目标"，可能变成下一阶段的"代价"（先是污染，后是房地产）。作为中国的投资者或企业家，你总想知道自己是国家目标的"目标"还是"代价"——当那个"代价"很不好受。总的结果是：一个体量巨大的国家终于踏上了完整的现代化进程——但代价是二元经济、信心流失、股市停滞的十年，以及一些具有世界级竞争力的公司以便宜的价格交易。对那些着眼全局的理性投资者来说，今天的市场也许提供了比其他时候更好的机会。

That's the development model: every ~10 years a new set of objectives with a heavy cost, and this period's objective can become the next period's cost (pollution, then real estate). As an investor or entrepreneur in China, you always want to know whether you're the objective or the cost — being the cost of a state objective is no fun. The net result: a country of great size finally embarked on a complete course of modernization — but with a two-tiered economy, lost confidence, a flat decade for stocks, and some world-class companies trading at cheap prices. For rational investors who look at the whole picture, today's market may offer better opportunities than other times.

1:15:02

中国与硅谷做AI的不同路径Chinese vs. Silicon Valley approach to AI

硅谷的路径问的是：这项技术还能做什么、我们能把边界推到多远——这就是他们自称"实验室""前沿"的原因。中国的AI公司对这些问题同样充满好奇，但更重要的是会问：这项技术能为我的客户——无论是消费者还是企业客户——做什么？这是一条很不同的路径，从各种指标上都能看到结果的不同。

The Silicon Valley approach asks: what else can this technology do, how far can we push the edge — that's why they're "labs" and "frontier." Chinese AI companies are curious about that too, but more importantly ask: what can this technology do for my customers, both consumers and enterprises? That's a very different approach, visible in different metrics and results.

1:16:04

什么来接替房地产成为家庭财富What replaces real estate as household wealth

当房地产在国家和家庭资产负债表中的占比下降，必然要有别的东西接替它，成为财富的载体。到某个时点，那只能是股权——它是唯一随经济一起增长的资产，实际上是因经济增长而增值，同时它本身又是经济增长的原因之一。它们同属一个飞轮。至于政治风险：要真正理解某一时刻的"目标"，考察历史记录——并把这个视角应用到你所进入的每一个国家，因为我们的世界仍然以民族国家为单位来组织。

As real estate's share of the national and family balance sheet declines, something must take its place as the store of wealth. At some point it has to be equities — the only asset that grows with the economy, in fact grows because of the economy and is itself a reason the economy grows. They're all part of the same flywheel. On political risk: understand the objective at any given time, examine the record — and apply that lens to every country you operate in, since our world is still organized around nation-states.

1:32:04

价值原则、成长与科技、能源——以及加密货币Value principle, growth and tech, energy — and crypto

所有理性的投资本质上都是价值投资，因为你总想得到比所付价格多一点的价值——无论是一家停滞甚至萎缩、但能返还大量现金的企业，还是一家快速成长的公司，又或是一笔胜率很小、但一旦成功回报巨大的押注。归根结底，都是在对未来的现金流做折现，并理解价值在何处被创造；基本原则不变，变的只是你如何应用它——而这取决于你的能力圈在哪里。能源、交通、制造、通信、知识型服务，本质上就是整个经济，所以AI会在每一层上复现；它的约束从芯片到电力到人力，各层表现不同，并且对气候有影响。

All intelligent investment is value investment, because you always want a bit more value than the price you pay — whether in a stagnant or declining business that returns a lot of cash, a rapidly growing company, or a bet with a small probability of a large payoff. It all comes down to discounting future cash flows and understanding where value is created; the basic principle doesn't change, only how you apply it — which depends on your circle of competence. Energy, transportation, manufacturing, communication, and knowledge-based services essentially are the whole economy, so AI replicates across every layer; its constraints run from chips to electricity to labor, and it has climate implications.

至于加密货币，我的回答很简单：我根本不去想它；我认为它最终会自行消失。它从根本上是站不住脚的。可兑现的货币，是靠其背后的强制力来支撑的；没有那种强制机制，你就无法拥有一个可信的结算单位或财富储藏手段——某个人"在车库里"造出来的东西，做不到这件事。它确实帮助了犯罪分子进行各种非法交易。我们干脆禁掉它，大家都会更好。如果有人想像收藏艺术品那样去收藏它，那是个人偏好，但作为社会，我认为它毫无价值。

On crypto, a simple answer: I don't think anything of it; I think it will eventually disappear on its own. It's fundamentally bogus. A redeemable currency is enforced by the force behind it; without that enforcement mechanism you can't have a credible unit of settlement or store of wealth — something created "out of a garage" doesn't do the job. It has helped criminals facilitate illicit transactions. We're all better off banning it. If someone wants to collect it like art, that's a personal preference, but as a society I think nothing of it.

1:36:07

连续两年跑输；指数 vs 坚持Two years of underperformance; index vs. conviction

真正的问题在于人性：你既想做正和的、长期正确的事，又想交出漂亮的数字——每年、每季、每月、每天都想——很快你就变成了投机者。你没法两头都要。要么你认为市场是来"指导"你的，要么认为市场是来"服务"你的——你只能二选一。想把这一切都糅在一起的人，不如去买指数，因为指数恰恰是全部四类参与者的平均。要把它做成一门事业，你最终需要志同道合、愿意与你坚持下去的人；而且你得有点逆向，带着某种"被屏蔽的基因"，仅因逻辑和事实如此就相信自己是对的，而不在乎别人是否同意。

The real issue is human psychology: you want to do win-win and long-term right things, but you also want to report good numbers — every year, quarter, month, day — and pretty soon you're a speculator. You can't have it all. Either you think the market is there to instruct you, or to serve you — you can only pick one. Anyone trying to mush it all together is better off buying the index, which is exactly the average of all four participant types. To do this as a business you need like-minded people who'll stick with you; and you have to be somewhat contrarian, with enough of a "muted gene" to believe you're right because logic and facts say so, regardless of whether others agree.

1:38:48

安全边际、复利知识，与过好一生Margin of safety, compounding knowledge, and a life well lived

价值投资者区别于多数投资者的，是对下行保护的极度重视。既然未来不可预测，你就投资于你确实了解的东西——而起步时你了解得很少。我20多岁起步时懂得很少，于是从我能理解的东西入手（资产负债表——格雷厄姆的方法），再逐渐从简单走向复杂。这就是能力圈的自然演进：理解它的边界，并有意识地随时间扩大它。如果你能以可持续的方式复利你的知识，最终你也能复利你的资本。

What distinguishes a value investor is enormous attention to downside protection. Since the future is unpredictable, you invest in what you do know — and early on you know little. I started in my 20s knowing very little, so I went with what I could understand (the balance sheet — Graham's approach), then gradually progressed from simple to complex. That's the natural progression of the circle of competence: understand its edge, and consciously expand it over time. If you can compound your knowledge sustainably, eventually you can compound your capital as well.

每过10年，我都发现自己学到了太多，几乎像换了一个人。选择正和而非零和，能让你复利你的能力、知识——甚至你的健康，因为你保持投入、保持有用，而不是从社会中索取。麻烦在于，人性往往滑向零和与短期。我的导师、合伙人查理·芒格一直倡导：最高层次的关系，是一张由彼此值得信赖所织成的无缝之网。那里面蕴含着一整套富有成效、对社会双赢的人生哲学。

Every 10 years I find I've learned so much I'm almost a different person. Choosing win-win over zero-sum lets you compound your ability, knowledge — and perhaps even your health, because you stay engaged and useful rather than taking from society. The trouble is human nature drifts to zero-sum and the short term. My mentor and partner Charlie Munger always advocated: the highest order of relationship is a seamless web of deserved trust. There lies a whole philosophy of a productive, win-win life.

复利最基本的理念是：别丢掉你已经拥有的——你的资本、你的知识、你值得信赖的关系、你的智慧，甚至你的健康。如果你选对了伙伴、选对了方法、选对了可押注的人和事，时间会让复利的效果变得惊人。在这一点上，美国与中国——以及全人类——共享同样的结构。找到你天然被吸引的事，以复利、累积的方式在其上不断建设。这就是过一种富有成效的人生的、更好的方式。非常感谢大家。

The basic idea of compounding is: don't lose what you already have — your capital, your knowledge, your trusting relationships, your wisdom, even your health. If you choose the right partners, the right approach, the right people and things to bet on, it's remarkable how compounding works over time. On that regard, the US and China — and all of humanity — share the same structure. Find something you're naturally drawn to and build on it in a compounding, cumulative fashion. That's just a better way of living a productive life. Thank you so much.

PART 02

核心观点

01
政治秩序在变，统一市场会留下来

政治秩序的形成与转变以十年、百年计；在它之下，经济秩序几百年来稳步成形，约 30 年前随冷战结束最终合拢为一个共同市场。市场带来效率和持续改进的飞轮，任何国家长期退出都代价极大。

相互依赖只增不减：生成式 AI 离不开的芯片，产业链横跨中美两端。所以双方在激烈竞争的同时维持和平，各自相信时间站在自己这边。

02
财富，是你对全球购买力的占比

这是喜马拉雅资本给价值投资加上的第六条原则。作为全球投资者，实现它的方式是：在有活力的国家和市场里，以有利的价格买入伟大的企业并长期持有，在创造价值的同时保持并提升这个占比。

03
教堂旁边的赌场：正和还是零和

借巴菲特的比喻，股市是一座附带赌场的教堂。伯克希尔用 60 年把教堂建成了大教堂；李录想证明这套“福音”能传到亚洲。投机按定义停留不够久，参与不了价值创造，对社会近乎为零、甚至是成本。

赌场源于人性，无法消除；但个人可以选择站在哪一边，社会也可以选择不让赌场大到压过市场存在的本意。

04
AI 必须留在“工具”一侧

这一波技术不同于以往：我们第一次站在“工具与造物”的光谱上，并开始移动——从语言、推理、编程，到能自主执行、彼此协作的智能体。硅谷的叙事是一路走向“造神”，并邀请所有人参与“神的 IPO”。

李录的判断是：要么技术根本走不到那一步，要么人类会联合起来阻止它。社会必须划出红线——AI 的学习与使用不能脱离“人在环中”。

05
AI 拉平的，是经济的最顶层

他的临时定义：AI 用巨大算力和算法，在人类生成的全部数据之间建立关联，是这些数据的“一阶导数”。经济从下到上是能源交通、制造、通信与数字媒体、知识型服务；今天的 AI 主要在综合并拉平最顶层的知识与专长，改变不了原子与分子层面的东西。

它还始终带着增量成本，因此财务纪律一直起作用，社会也会经历不止一轮繁荣与崩溃。

06
在中国，先弄清你是“目标”还是“代价”

过去十年中国股市停滞，底下却发生了大迁移：为跨越中等收入陷阱，经济被有意从中低端推向中高端制造。代价主要落在中产家庭——房价下跌约四成，家庭财富缩水六七成，人们少消费、多储蓄、还债务。

与此同时，制造业升级提前完成目标，外部制裁反而为半导体创造了需求。每十年一组目标，这一阶段的目标可能变成下一阶段的代价；结果是二元经济与一批被低估的世界级公司。

07
市场会做怪事，长期你得到应得的

AI 资本开支占 GDP 的比重从历史上的 2%—4% 升到约 15%；一家自称有一成概率毁灭所有人的公司，邀请所有人以两万亿美元估值投入千亿。市场总会做出奇怪的事，但你不必被它牵着走。

标普 2010 年后年均约 14%—15%，2000—2010 年却接近零；拉长看是高个位数，大致等于 GDP 增长加通胀。这种两极分化给能保持理性的人更好的机会，前提是你真的能保持理性。

08
复利，从不丢掉已有的开始

未来不可预测，所以价值投资者格外重视下行保护：从自己确实了解的东西起步，认清能力圈的边界，再有意识地扩大它。如果你能以可持续的方式复利知识，最终也能复利资本。

选择正和而非零和，能让能力、知识甚至健康一起复利；别丢掉已经拥有的资本、知识、信任与健康，再选对伙伴和方法，时间会让复利的效果变得惊人。

核心观点

01
信息社会化：一门没有标准答案的课

社会化，是个人在与他人和环境的互动中习得知识、规范与价值观，进而承担社会角色。节目把它延伸为信息社会化：在一个由信息塑造认知与行为的环境里，学会获取、理解与拒绝信息，并保有判断生活和行动的主动权。

这道题没有一劳永逸的答案，人与信息的关系需要长期经营。

02
信息在增加，理解未必在增加

信息爆炸与信息茧房、情绪极化同时存在。节目借一份“思想验证”小测验提醒：我们很少主动检验自己的观点。信息变多了，事实与理解不一定随之变多。

03
注意力正在被算法和 AI 分配

决定我们看到什么的，是被称作“时代最大科技黑箱”的算法；当 AI 开始占据信息流，注意力被引导甚至被“做局”。节目延伸推荐的一篇文章，讨论的正是把算法控制权交还给用户的可能。

04
公共议题为何越来越像饭圈

信息的流通方式也在塑造公共参与：越来越多公共事件被以“饭圈”的逻辑理解——先认阵营，再看事实。面对复杂议题，站队往往比严谨更快、也更有回报。

05
从生产者看：新闻死了，还是太长不看

换到内容生产者的位置，问题变成：是新闻本身在式微，还是读者不再愿意读完长内容？当完整的报道没人读完，复杂性就最先被牺牲。

06
远方的事、发言的资格与心力的耗竭

当事人离自己很远，舆论却让人感觉无法独善其身；随之而来的是两个难题：如何判断一个人发言的合法性，惩罚的强度又该如何界定。

持续的卷入最终带来一种耗竭：内心已经没有容纳公共议题的余地。

07
主动给自己建一个“茧房”

与其被动地被算法包裹，不如主动设计自己的信息环境：为单位时间里摄取的信息设定主题，减少对社交网络上他人私生活的关注，把注意力留给自己真正想理解的事。

08
表达、判断与参与，都要有边界

为互联网表达设定边界，把公共性与私人性分开；把“验证”当成一个需要主动去做的动作，而不是默认会发生的事。最后落在一句：关心世界，未必需要用激烈的方式。

02

编码更快后，评审、持续集成、问题分诊和发布立刻成为新瓶颈。局部提速如果没有配套系统，最终只会制造更大的等待队列。

03

过去为新人上手而写的说明，如今也成为智能体理解代码库的入口。轻量的仓库指南应当像活文档一样，随着 AI 犯错持续补充。

04
把专家知识编码成可复用技能

无障碍、性能、安全等知识不应永远依赖少数专家反复答疑。把规则沉淀为技能，能让专家从重复咨询中释放出来，也让标准更稳定。

05
AI 必须能够看见自己做出的结果

模型说界面“看起来正确”没有意义。组件截图、浏览器检查和自动启动流程，让智能体获得视觉反馈，从生成代码走向自我纠错。

06
评审是风险闸门，不是事后过滤器

不同仓库应采用不同强度的 AI 评审，但自动意见处理完后仍需人类判断。人负责最终责任，AI 负责扩大检查覆盖面。

07
真实问题应该自动进入评测集

评测不能长期停留在最初的固定样例。每次线上问题都应沉淀为新的场景，使系统在真实失败中持续学习。

08
原型的价值是开启更具体的对话

不必把每个想法都做成可合并的正式改动。一个能运行的粗糙原型，就能让讨论从抽象想象转向具体取舍，帮助团队更快达成共识。
